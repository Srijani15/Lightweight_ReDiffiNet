`dcd3ee1e814cd0628bdc7289576926bfa652f9e3` of the supplied checkout.

## Dataset and exact evaluation contract

Disk files are trusted pickle/gzip tuples:
`(t1c, t1n, t2f, t2w, ground_truth, frozen_hard_labels)`.
Both label maps are uint8 0/1/2/3. The loader makes network input
`[t1c,t1n,t2f,t2w,WT,TC,ET]`, where WT={1,2,3}, TC={1,3}, ET={3}.
Ground truth is returned separately and never concatenated into inference input.

MRI arrays already contain the U-Net preprocessing: nonzero-voxel z-normalization,
the existing intensity rescaling, and the saved deterministic spatial crop.
The historical fixed crop is [56:184,24:216,14:142]; an explicit saved crop overrides
it. The loader does not normalize, reorder, resize or crop the export again.
Training alone uses uniform, label-independent random patches. Validation
covers the entire exported crop through CPU-stitched sliding-window inference;
it is not claimed to evaluate voxels outside the U-Net crop.

Evaluation exactly follows the inspected optimized U-Net:
- WT >0.45, TC >0.40, ET >0.45.
- Disjoint label conversion constrains TC/ET to WT; ET overrides the TC decision.
- Accumulate float64 intersections and volumes across all validation patients.
- Dice=(2*intersection+1e-8)/(prediction_size+target_size+1e-8).
- Both-empty region Dice is 1. Mean Dice is the arithmetic mean of WT/TC/ET Dice.
- Per-case scores are also exported, but **their arithmetic average is not the
  checkpoint-selection score**. HD95 and validation loss are not fabricated.

Hard-label export already applies these exact thresholds/conversions. Converting
those labels back to regions loses probability confidence but should preserve
the binary masks and Dice when inference outputs match. A discrepancy cannot
automatically be attributed to hard labels. The U-Net exporter runs FP32 without
autocast; BF16 checkpoint validation may produce slightly different decisions
near thresholds. Also inspect the generating checkpoint, crop and patient set.
The verifier prints signed differences from checkpoint metrics and the reported
fold-0 epoch-20 score, and records pooled counts through the metric implementation.
Numerical differences >1e-8 require explicit review before a diagnostic is enabled.

## One residual diffusion formulation

Let b be the three hard U-Net regions, y the target, and r0=y-b in [-1,1].
The retained 1,000-step linear Gaussian schedule samples

`r_t = sqrt(alpha_bar_t)*r0 + sqrt(1-alpha_bar_t)*epsilon`.

The original BasicUNetEncoder conditions the original BasicUNetDe at five scales.
The denoiser receives seven condition channels plus three noisy residual channels,
with the original sinusoidal timestep embedding. Its output is decoded as

`r_hat = (1-2*b) * sigmoid(magnitude_logits)`.

For binary b, this allows every necessary correction: positive for a false
negative and negative for a false positive. It also keeps composition inside
[0,1] without an outward residual getting stuck behind clamp's zero gradient.

A three-channel 1x1 gate head on the conditioning encoder's first feature map
produces `gate=sigmoid(gate_logits)`. It sees MRI and b only, never y or
the label-noised diffusion target. It estimates baseline error probability;
it is **learned uncertainty, not recovered U-Net probability entropy**.

`final = clamp(b + gate*r_hat, 0, 1)`

The primary loss is soft Dice plus BCE on final. Auxiliary losses are residual
MSE to y-b (weight 0.5), gate BCE to abs(y-b) (0.1), and correction magnitude in
already-correct regions (0.05). These explicit starting weights are hypotheses,
not experimentally optimized values. Both output heads start at bias -2 to favor
small corrections while retaining gradients. Final BCE/loss calculations are FP32.

DDIM starts from Gaussian residual noise and repeatedly calls the trained model.
START_X means exactly the signed residual during both training and sampling.
There is no sigmoid applied to a signed x0 after sampling. The gate is applied
after residual sampling. Training supervises one uniformly sampled noisy timestep,
as in denoising training; validation uses the complete reverse trajectory.
Identical mathematical decoding does not guarantee identical train/sample quality.

Intentional upstream differences:
- Original noised signed segmentation masks, trained raw outputs as logits, then
  treated those logits as x0 during sampling. The signed-residual target fixes
  that mismatch, rather than calling a CNN once and labelling it diffusion.
- Correct WT/TC naming and pooled U-Net metric aggregation.
- Replace seed-42 ad hoc splitting with mandatory authoritative assignments.
- Width defaults to 32 (original 64), preserving depth and block types. This
  reduces convolution cost/capacity; quality impact needs measurement. Width 64
  remains selectable and needs its own diagnostic.
- Fixed forward schedule remains 1,000 steps; configurable DDIM defaults to 20
  instead of assuming 50 is optimal. The original progressive sampler is reused
  without retaining/copying all intermediate volumes.
- Dynamic batch/patch shapes replace the hard-coded (1,3,128,192,128) sample shape.

## Training and outputs

Provisional first configuration: width 32, batch 1, 96^3 training patches and
inference windows, BF16 if supported, AdamW lr=1e-4/weight_decay=1e-3, 20 DDIM steps,
validation every two epochs, maximum 60 epochs. Retain the original optimizer
learning rate/decay while first testing the refinement formulation. Patch size,
width and fewer reverse steps reduce costs but require empirical validation;
60 is a ceiling, not a promised required duration. ReduceLROnPlateau halves LR
after its patience=2 criterion; early stopping uses six validation checks without
a >1e-4 improvement. The true maximum validation Mean Dice always selects best.pt,
independent of the early-stopping tolerance.

Workers default to 2, prefetch 1, pinned CUDA transfers. Workers restart per epoch
so seeded patch/shuffle order resumes reproducibly. Full volumes and stitching
stay on CPU; one inference patch is sent to GPU. No model graph, histograms, MRI
batches or per-batch TensorBoard scalars are logged.

```text
ReDiffiNet_Output/
  audit/fold_0/{verification.json,baseline_per_case.csv}
  diagnostic/fold_0/{checkpoints/,tensorboard/,evaluation/,diagnostic.json,run_config.json}
  fold_0/
    checkpoints/{best_mean_dice.pt,latest.pt}
    tensorboard/
    evaluation/{best_per_case.csv,best_metrics.json}
    run_config.json
  fold_1/ ... fold_4/             # only when run independently
  cross_validation_summary.csv   # completed research folds only
```

Checkpoints atomically save model/optimizer/scheduler/scaler, RNG state, fold,
epoch, configuration, best metrics, stopping state, history, duration, data/code
identity and dependency versions. Resume rejects mismatches. Interruptions replay
the current epoch from the last completed checkpoint; they do not claim to resume
at an arbitrary minibatch. An initial epoch-0 checkpoint supports interruption
during epoch 1. A larger epoch ceiling may be supplied on resume; other saved
configuration must match. Early-stopped runs remain stopped.

Diagnostic outputs never enter the research summary. A successful CUDA diagnostic
for matching data/code/width/patch/ROI/AMP/batch/overlap/determinism/dependencies is required for
full training. A diagnostic cannot prove long-run quality or avoid every OOM.
Validation uses fixed per-patient seeds and restores training RNG state.
Cross-fold reporting uses unweighted mean and population standard deviation
(ddof=0) of completed folds' pooled Dice, explicitly stating whether all five exist.
The original U-Net summary contains per-fold rows; it does not establish a std
convention, so ddof=0 is an explicit added reporting choice.

### CUDA determinism policy and failed diagnostic recovery

`--determinism warn-only` is the default for the college CUDA environment.
The retained MaxPool3d backward has no deterministic CUDA implementation in the
reported server environment, so strict mode stops during backward. Policies:

- `strict`: `torch.use_deterministic_algorithms(True, warn_only=False)`.
- `warn-only`: `torch.use_deterministic_algorithms(True, warn_only=True)`;
  unsupported operations run with warnings. **This does not guarantee fully
  deterministic CUDA execution or bitwise reproducibility after resume.**
- `off`: `torch.use_deterministic_algorithms(False, warn_only=False)`.

All modes retain the existing seeds, RNG checkpoint/restore, fixed validation
sampling seeds, TF32 settings, disabled cuDNN benchmarking and cuDNN deterministic
preference. `off` disables the global algorithm enforcement, not these mechanisms.
The policy is stored in run configuration, checkpoints and diagnostic reports.
Resume requires matching configuration; full runs require a diagnostic with the
same policy. Evaluation uses the checkpoint's policy with no silent override.
The legacy `light_training/trainer.py` MONAI seed call is unused by this pipeline.

The first backward occurs AFTER writing run_config.json and an initial epoch-0
latest.pt and opening TensorBoard. A fresh run that failed there is expected to
have these artifacts, but no best checkpoint or successful diagnostic.json.
The remote files cannot be inspected from the local editing workspace. To inspect
them read-only on the server (adjust the original output root if needed):

```bash

```

## Local validation and remaining limits

### Inspecting a zero-Dice-delta diagnostic without retraining

Source review and synthetic CPU checks confirm the refinement path is functional.
The final-segmentation loss alone reaches the gate, denoiser output, early/deep
denoiser blocks, timestep embedding and conditioning encoder. Sampling calls the
learned denoiser at each DDIM step and uses its signed residual in the final output.
No unintended detach, channel swap, masking error or inference bypass was found.
No architecture/loss/optimizer/sampler changes were needed.

For a hard baseline b, the correction magnitude is u=gate*sigmoid(residual_logits).
A zero baseline becomes positive only when u exceeds the threshold: .45 WT/ET,
.40 TC. A one baseline turns off when u reaches .55 WT/ET or .60 TC. The existing
region hierarchy still applies, so some individual channel changes can be masked
or propagated by postprocessing. Both heads start at bias -2, making u about
sigmoid(-2)^2=0.0142 before feature contributions. This is deliberately below all
threshold crossings, with nonzero sigmoid derivatives. Two epochs on eight cases
(16 optimizer steps at batch size 1) can alter probabilities without changing
binary masks. Gate/error imbalance can also favor preserving the baseline; actual
checkpoint statistics are needed before attributing the observed zero delta.
Equal Dice alone does not prove masks are identical: edits can preserve counts.

Optional `inspect_refinement.py` performs read-only inference on the saved model.
It retains all current checkpoint/fold/data/source/dependency checks and uses the
saved determinism, seed, AMP, ROI and DDIM steps. It prints per-case:

- Mean/max gate and absolute residual, after Gaussian stitching over unique
  cropped voxels. The final probability is stitched directly; it is not rebuilt
  from the separately stitched gate and residual.
- Mean absolute final-minus-baseline probability difference.
- Changed fractions for binary region decisions (denominator 3*X*Y*Z), per-region
  decisions, and spatial voxels where any postprocessed region changes.
- Corrected/worsened percentages among changed decisions. For spatial voxels,
  an exact-target correction, a regression from exact correctness and a change
  from one wrong label to another are distinguished. Zero changes give null
  percentages, not a fabricated 0% correction rate.

Copy only the new inspector (and optional tests/documentation) to the server;
